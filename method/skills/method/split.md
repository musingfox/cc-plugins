# Do or split

Do the contract yourself. That is the default.

Split only for one of three reasons:

- **It does not fit one context.** The work, read and built in one session, would outrun your window.
- **It has parts that can run in parallel.** Two or more pieces touch disjoint files and none consumes another's interface.
- **A part needs a different model.** One piece hinges on a hard judgment, or is mechanical enough for a cheaper seat (the model table in `muster/skills/muster/SKILL.md`).

Size, importance and tidiness are not reasons. Every extra layer costs a context: an estimated $5–7 per case for a main session alone, before its review.

## You do it: a leaf

Build it. Then run the mechanical gates over your own commits and quote their verdict lines in your report (`review.md` "Leaf"). No review is dispatched for a leaf. Report with `report.md`.

## You split: an author

Splitting makes you the **author** of the sub-contracts. You own:

1. Writing them, and a `contracts.json` per sub-node: `contracts.md`.
2. Dispatching them: `dispatch.md`.
3. Answering what they report: `route.md`.
4. Merging their branches into your own, so your branch is the **integrated branch**.
5. Reporting `DONE` up. Your author then dispatches the one review of your split, against your contract, over your integrated branch, with your sub-contracts attached (`review.md`).

Split once, at the grain the reason demands. A part that turns out too big for its node splits again there, by that node's own judgment.

Done: you named which of the three reasons holds, or you are building it yourself.
